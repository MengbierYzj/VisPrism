# Dallas Morning News

Source: https://policyviz.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/tdmn_graphics.pdf  
Readable transcription mirror: https://www.readkong.com/page/the-dallas-morning-news-graphics-stylebook-5688204

## Scope

The Dallas Morning News Graphics Stylebook (October 2005) is a newsroom graphics identity guide. It combines editorial principles with concrete rules for typography, spacing, color, charts, maps, workflow, and credits.

## Editorial philosophy

- Aim for simplicity, usefulness, and clarity; avoid gimmicks. When in doubt, choose the simplest solution.
- Increase white space. If there is too much information, make the graphic larger; if space is tight, remove content rather than compressing it.
- Keep text short so the graphic is faster and more attractive to read. The guide is a strong but flexible set of rules, not an inflexible “bible.”

## Typography and hierarchy

- Graphic headlines are uppercase Gotham Condensed Bold. The size scales with width: 16 pt for 1–2 columns, 20 pt for 3–4 columns, and 24 pt for 5–6 columns.
- Use Gotham Book for intro text, source lines, long callouts, labels, and map/river/landmark text; Gotham Bold for subheads and callouts. Avoid overly condensed or ultra-heavy Gotham variants at small sizes.
- Keep callouts prominent but limited. A long callout should be no more than about 20 words/130 characters; never solve crowding by making type illegibly small.
- Use short type boxes, break long copy with subheads, and avoid wrapping text around illustrations.

## Spacing and graphic boxes

- Use consistent box padding and headline hierarchy. Graphic boxes use thin black rules; do not use a box when the headline already uses the designated border treatment.
- White space and alignment are treated as core readability features, not decoration. Avoid compressing excessive content into a small canvas.

## Color

- The stylebook provides a controlled palette for fever, bar, and pie charts, plus map colors. Use the palette consistently rather than improvising arbitrary hues.
- Color should support category, emphasis, and geographic distinction while keeping labels and data readable. Avoid using too many callout colors or colors that obscure the map/plot.

## Chart forms

- **Fever/line charts:** emphasize the trend and use restrained supporting marks and labels.
- **Bar charts:** use clear baselines, readable labels, and the newsroom palette; prioritize direct comparison over decoration.
- **Pie charts and tables:** reserve pie charts for appropriate part-to-whole stories and keep table typography/spacing aligned with the overall graphic system.
- **Maps:** use a consistent map-element vocabulary, locator maps, state maps, and world/earth maps. Apply a stable hierarchy for water, land, boundaries, labels, and pointers.

## Workflow and credits

- The guide includes a workflow for gathering resources, coordinating with editors/reporters, and checking graphics before publication. Credit graphics and source material clearly.
- Templates and base maps are resources, not substitutes for editorial judgment; adapt them when the story requires it while preserving the core identity.

## Implementation notes for an agent

For a Dallas Morning News-like graphic, use a clean editorial hierarchy: uppercase condensed headline, ample white space, short explanatory text, restrained palette, clear source/credit line, and a chart/map form selected for the story rather than novelty.

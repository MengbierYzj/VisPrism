## When to use a Bar or Column Chart
» Use mostly for one variable.
» Compares numerical values for different 
observations. Shows relative amounts.
» Grouped or stacked bars or columns can break 
that one numerical variable out into different 
 sub-groups.

 ## When to use a Line Graph
» Shows the trend in one variable usually over time. 
» Multiple lines can show multiple variables (if they 
are on the same scale).
» Multiple lines can also show the same variable for 
multiple observations.

## When to use a Pie Chart
» Use them very sparingly. Often a bar or column 
chart is better. It is much more difficult to visually 
judge the size of circles (or circle segments) vs. 
rectangles. 
» You want to show the relative relationship 
between 2-3 things.
» They add up to 100% (which may necessitate the 
inclusion of a category such as “none”, “other”, etc)

## When to use a Scatter Plot
» Shows the relationship between two continuous 
variables for your set of observations.
» Each point in the plot represents an object.
» You can change color or symbol to show groups.
» Sometimes it is nice to show a trend line 
(regression).
